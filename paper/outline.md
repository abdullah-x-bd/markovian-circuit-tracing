# Paper outline

## Working title

Markovian Circuit Tracing

## One-line claim

We can recover and causally test probabilistic state-transition structure inside small transformer circuits when the data-generating latent dynamics are known.

## Abstract skeleton

Mechanistic interpretability often produces local explanations for individual prompts. Many behaviors, however, unfold as trajectories through internal states. We introduce Markovian Circuit Tracing, a method for recovering probabilistic transition structure among internal feature states. We validate the method on transformers trained on sequences generated by known Hidden Markov Models, where the true latent transition matrix and Bayes optimal next-token predictions are available. We show that internal state abstractions recovered from activations can approximate the true transition law, satisfy order-1 Markov tests better than weaker baselines, and support exact counterfactual evaluation through state forcing. This gives a controlled benchmark for studying when circuit explanations can be represented as reusable state machines rather than prompt-local graphs.

- The key validation benchmark uses known HMM dynamics.

### Method

- Define internal state extractor g(a)
- Estimate transition matrix over discovered states
- Test Markov order
- Compare recovered transitions to known latent transitions
- Force internal states and compare outputs to exact counterfactual targets

### Synthetic benchmark

- Four-state HMM
- Six visible tokens
- Tiny causal transformer
- Bayes optimal predictor

### Results

- Training reaches near-Bayes loss
- State recovery from activations
- Internal transition matrix recovery
- Markov property tests
- Forced-state counterfactual results
- Baseline comparison

### Discussion

- What this proves
- What it does not prove

### Limitations

- Synthetic HMMs are simpler than language
- Learned representations may not be uniquely identifiable
- Markovian abstractions can fail when the model stores long history
- SAE quality may shape the result

### Conclusion

Markovian Circuit Tracing offers a way to turn circuit explanations into testable probabilistic state machines. The HMM benchmark gives exact ground truth and causal counterfactual targets, making it a useful validation setting for future mechanistic interpretability methods.
